# FINAL QUICK-REFERENCE SECTION

## The 20 Most Dangerous Anorectal Mistakes

| No. | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Calling anorectal bleeding “piles” without inspection, DRE, and anoscopy. |
| 2 | Treating bleeding haemorrhoids in a patient with iron-deficiency anaemia or other alarm features before the colon is examined. |
| 3 | Discharging a febrile patient with perianal pain on antibiotics alone. |
| 4 | Waiting for fluctuance before draining an abscess. |
| 5 | Missing an intersphincteric abscess because the skin looked normal. |
| 6 | Delaying Fournier’s surgery for CT, MRI, or a score. |
| 7 | Missing the SGLT2-inhibitor link and euglycaemic ketoacidosis. |
| 8 | Discharging the post-banding pelvic sepsis triad as “discomfort”. |
| 9 | Banding irreducible, thrombosed, or external tissue — or rectal varices. |
| 10 | Prescribing GTN to a patient using a PDE5 inhibitor or riociguat. |
| 11 | Sphincterotomy or fistulotomy in an atypical fissure, complex fistula, or active Crohn’s disease. |
| 12 | Repeating steroid creams for a non-healing lesion and missing anal cancer. |
| 13 | Excising, banding, or cauterising a suspicious lesion without histology. |
| 14 | Giving loperamide for overflow from faecal impaction. |
| 15 | Routine rectal instrumentation in profound neutropenia — or letting that rule delay surgical review. |
| 16 | Reducing gangrenous prolapsed bowel. |
| 17 | Pulling a foreign body against a contracted sphincter, breaking glass, or grasping drug packets. |
| 18 | Missing STI proctitis (including LGV) or HIV because no sexual history was taken. |
| 19 | Delaying HIV PEP or emergency contraception after sexual assault. |
| 20 | Missing cauda equina syndrome, intussusception, or a safeguarding concern presenting as an “anal” problem. |

## The 20 Red Flags That Require Same-Day Escalation

| No. | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Perianal pain with a temperature above 38.0 °C. |
| 2 | Severe rectal pain with fever and normal-looking skin. |
| 3 | Perineal pain out of proportion, crepitus, dusky skin, or bullae. |
| 4 | Perineal infection in a patient with diabetes, immunosuppression, or an SGLT2 inhibitor. |
| 5 | Worsening pain, fever, or urinary difficulty after banding or anorectal surgery. |
| 6 | Bleeding soaking more than 1 pad per hour, or clots, after a procedure. |
| 7 | Shock index > 1, syncope, or haemodynamic instability with rectal bleeding. |
| 8 | Inability to pass urine for 6–8 hours after a procedure. |
| 9 | Prolapsed rectum or haemorrhoids that are dusky, black, or irreducible. |
| 10 | Rectal foreign body with peritonism, free air, a sharp object, or drug packets. |
| 11 | Abdominal pain, distension, or fever in a patient with faecal impaction. |
| 12 | Perianal pain or fever in a neutropenic patient. |
| 13 | New faecal incontinence with back pain, saddle numbness, or urinary retention. |
| 14 | Sexual assault within 72 hours (HIV PEP) or 120 hours (emergency contraception). |
| 15 | A child with redcurrant-jelly stool, colicky pain, and lethargy. |
| 16 | A child with bloody diarrhoea and pallor or reduced urine output. |
| 17 | Any safeguarding concern in a child or vulnerable adult. |
| 18 | Perianal abscess in pregnancy. |
| 19 | Perianal sepsis in known Crohn’s disease or in a patient on biologics. |
| 20 | Severe constipation with abdominal pain in a patient taking clozapine. |

## The 10 Rules for Safe Anorectal Examination

| No. | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Check Appendix C before touching the rectum. |
| 2 | Explain, obtain consent, and offer a chaperone. |
| 3 | Position well: left lateral at the edge of the couch, with good light. |
| 4 | Look before you feel — inspect at rest and on straining. |
| 5 | Give lidocaine gel 5–10 minutes to work. |
| 6 | Never force — stop and book EUA if pain prevents a safe examination. |
| 7 | Record resting tone, squeeze, and push separately. |
| 8 | Use anoscopy to see internal haemorrhoids. |
| 9 | Examine both groins when you find a lesion. |
| 10 | Document by anatomical quadrant and distance from the verge — never by clock face alone. |

## The 10 Essential Referral Triggers

| No. | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Any same-day red flag above → emergency or same-day surgical review. |
| 2 | Alarm features or a positive FIT → colon evaluation (Chapter 08). |
| 3 | Anal mass, non-healing ulcer or plaque, or inguinal nodes → suspected-cancer pathway (Chapter 07). |
| 4 | Atypical fissure, recurrent abscess, or complex fistula → colorectal surgery ± gastroenterology (Chapter 09). |
| 5 | Chronic fissure not healed after 6–8 weeks of medical therapy → surgical clinic. |
| 6 | Haemorrhoids failing 4–6 weeks of conservative care → office-procedure clinic. |
| 7 | STI proctitis or anorectal ulcer → sexual-health service (Chapter 11). |
| 8 | Faecal incontinence after stepped care, or after OASIS → pelvic-floor or colorectal service (Chapters 10, 13). |
| 9 | Recurrent rectal prolapse in a child → paediatrics and sweat test (Chapter 14). |
| 10 | Persistent symptoms when examination is impossible → examination under anaesthesia. |
