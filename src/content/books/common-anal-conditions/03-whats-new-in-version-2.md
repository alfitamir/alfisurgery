# WHAT'S NEW IN VERSION 2

Version 2.0 is a full clinical and editorial revision. Every Version 1 chapter has been re-checked against current guidance; claims that could not be traced to a source have been corrected, softened, or labelled. Nine new chapters extend the handbook from benign proctology into the conditions non-surgeons most often **miss**: anal cancer, colorectal cancer behind rectal bleeding, perianal Crohn’s disease, continence and pelvic-floor disorders, sexually transmitted proctitis, trauma and sexual assault, pregnancy, children, and rare but dangerous mimics. Six practical appendices, one-page patient handouts, and a final quick-reference section complete the edition.

## Clinically Significant Corrections Since Version 1

Readers who used Version 1 should note the following changes. Each is applied throughout Version 2.

**Table F.3 — Safety-relevant corrections and clarifications**

| Topic                                    | Version 1 wording                                                                                                                     | Version 2 position and reason                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SGLT2 inhibitors and Fournier’s gangrene | “FDA black-box warning”; “stop permanently”                                                                                           | The FDA issued a **2018 Drug Safety Communication and labelling warning** — not a boxed warning. Stop the drug immediately when Fournier’s gangrene is suspected; any restart is a specialist decision.                                                                                |
| Thrombosed external haemorrhoid          | “Never incise”; excision is the only procedure                                                                                        | ASCRS 2024 suggests early **excision** in selected patients; the AGA 2026 expert review advises **incision and drainage**. Version 2 states the disagreement [U] and recommends excision of the clot and vessel where expertise exists, avoiding a small stab that leaves clot behind. |
| PPAC2 trial                              | “Packing doubled pain”                                                                                                                | Packing produced **higher** pain (mean maximum VAS 38.2 vs 28.2) without reducing fistula (15% vs 11%) or recurrence. Exact figures now quoted.                                                                                                                                        |
| Psyllium dose                            | “Psyllium 25–35 g/day”                                                                                                                | 25–35 g/day is the **total dietary fibre** target. Psyllium supplements are usually started at about 3.5 g once or twice daily and titrated, with adequate fluid.                                                                                                                      |
| AGA 2026 citations                       | Attributed several thresholds to AGA without context                                                                                  | Now matched to the published best-practice advice: topical steroids ≤ 2 weeks; IRC preferred over banding in **significant coagulopathy** (platelets < 50 × 10⁹/L or INR > 2.0); defer haemorrhoid treatment in active IBD until remission. AGA 2026 is an ungraded expert review [C]. |
| BPRST citation                           | Wrong author list and year                                                                                                            | Corrected: Sobrado et al., Ann Med Surg 2021 (cohort and algorithm) and Ann Coloproctol 2020 (original classification).                                                                                                                                                                |
| Drug body-packers                        | “Drug packets → straight to theatre”                                                                                                  | Most asymptomatic body-packers are managed non-operatively with observation per toxicology protocol; surgery is for obstruction, perforation, or packet rupture with toxicity. Never grasp or puncture packets. Full guidance in Chapter 12.                                           |
| Sodium phosphate enemas                  | “Never in patients > 65”                                                                                                              | Avoid in older adults, CKD, dehydration, heart failure, bowel obstruction, and with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, or NSAIDs; never exceed one dose in 24 hours (FDA 2014 safety communication).                                                                                     |
| Neutropenia “Forbidden Rectum Rule”      | Presented as absolute                                                                                                                 | Retained as **expert consensus / local protocol** [C][L]. It forbids routine DRE, enemas, suppositories, and rectal thermometers — it **never** delays urgent surgical review or examination under anaesthesia when sepsis is suspected.                                               |
| Unsupported numbers                      | e.g. “90% diagnosed on inspection”, “teach-back halves readmissions”, “prolapse shrinks 30–50%”, “75% of pruritus is hygiene-related” | Removed or rewritten as qualitative statements. Reported figures without strong sources (for example Fournier’s spread rate) are labelled “reported”.                                                                                                                                  |
| Clock-face case                          | Contained contradictory orientation statements                                                                                        | Rewritten: clock positions depend on patient position **and** the examiner’s convention, so they are not interpretable without both — use anatomical quadrants.                                                                                                                        |
| Author’s surgical series                 | Cited without full reference                                                                                                          | Fully referenced and labelled as a single-centre case series (low certainty), for example Lasheen et al. 2016 (71 patients, 1 recurrence).                                                                                                                                             |

## How Evidence Strength Is Labelled

Every key recommendation carries a guideline or trial citation and, where helpful, one of four labels. The labels describe how firmly a statement is supported — not how important it is.

| Label              | Meaning                                                                                                     | Typical source                                                                              |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [S] Strong         | Evidence-based recommendation supported by graded guideline statements, randomised trials, or meta-analyses | ASCRS/ACG graded recommendations; PPAC2; Cochrane-level evidence                            |
| [C] Consensus      | Expert consensus, ungraded expert review, or established practice with limited trial data                   | AGA Clinical Practice Updates; WSES consensus; widely adopted bedside practice              |
| [L] Local protocol | Correct action depends on local policy, formulary, antibiogram, or service structure                        | Antibiotic choice, anticoagulation bridging, referral time targets, safeguarding pathways   |
| [U] Uncertain      | Evidence is limited or conflicting, or guidelines disagree                                                  | Thrombosed external haemorrhoid procedure choice; botulinum toxin dose; sitz-bath additives |

## Standard Referral Urgency Definitions (Used in Every Chapter)

| Category     | Definition                                                                                                                                                                    | Typical examples                                                                                                      |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 EMERGENCY | Call the on-call surgeon or senior now; resuscitate in parallel; theatre or examination under anaesthesia without delay. Do not wait for imaging if it delays source control. | Fournier’s gangrene, post-banding pelvic sepsis, haemodynamically unstable bleeding, gangrenous prolapse, perforation |
| 🔴 SAME-DAY  | Specialist assessment before the patient leaves hospital, or within hours.                                                                                                    | Perianal or intersphincteric abscess, incarcerated prolapse, acute urinary retention after a procedure                |
| 🟡 URGENT    | Specialist review within about 2 weeks, or through the national suspected-cancer pathway where one exists [L].                                                                | Suspected anal or colorectal cancer, atypical fissure, suspected perianal Crohn’s disease, positive FIT               |
| 🟢 ROUTINE   | Standard outpatient referral, or review in primary care at a stated interval.                                                                                                 | Grade II–III haemorrhoids failing 4–6 weeks of conservative care; chronic fissure failing 6–8 weeks of therapy        |

**Standard fever threshold:** throughout this handbook, “fever” in escalation rules and discharge advice means a **temperature above 38.0 °C**.

<small>Time targets vary between health systems. Where a national pathway exists (for example a suspected-cancer referral standard), it overrides the generic interval above.</small>

## Clinical-Use Disclaimer

> **⚠️ CLINICAL-USE DISCLAIMER**
>
> - This handbook is an educational aid for **qualified health professionals**. It summarises published guidance and expert practice as of the evidence-review date.
> - It **supports, but does not replace**, clinical judgement, local protocols, national formularies, and urgent specialist consultation. When this book and a local protocol differ, follow the local protocol and escalate the discrepancy.
> - Doses are typical adult doses unless stated. **Check every dose** against the current national formulary, renal and hepatic function, pregnancy and breastfeeding status, age, weight, allergies, and interactions before prescribing.
> - Nothing in this book should delay emergency resuscitation, surgical source control, safeguarding action, or referral of a suspected cancer.
