# APPENDIX B — MEDICATION SAFETY QUICK REFERENCE

<small>Typical adult doses. Check every dose against the national formulary, product labelling, renal and hepatic function, age, weight, pregnancy and breastfeeding status, and interactions [L].</small>

**Table B.1 — Indications, doses, and adverse effects**

| Medicine                                        | Indication                                                                                    | Usual adult dose                                                                                                                                                                                     | Key adverse effects                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Psyllium (ispaghula)                            | Constipation; haemorrhoids; fissure; firming loose stool                                      | About 3.5 g once or twice daily, titrated; each dose with ≥ 150 mL water                                                                                                                             | Bloating, flatulence; obstruction if fluid is inadequate                               |
| Macrogol (PEG 3350); lactulose; magnesium salts | Constipation; impaction (macrogol)                                                            | Macrogol 1–3 sachets daily (impaction regimen per formulary); lactulose 15 mL twice daily, titrated                                                                                                  | Bloating and cramps (lactulose); electrolyte disturbance; hypermagnesaemia (magnesium) |
| Stimulant laxatives (senna, bisacodyl)          | Constipation; opioid-induced constipation                                                     | Senna 7.5–15 mg at night; bisacodyl 5–10 mg at night                                                                                                                                                 | Cramps, diarrhoea, hypokalaemia with overuse                                           |
| Enemas and suppositories                        | Rectal loading; disimpaction                                                                  | Glycerol suppository; bisacodyl 10 mg suppository; tap-water or mineral-oil enema; sodium phosphate enema single dose only                                                                           | Rectal irritation; phosphate: hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, AKI                   |
| Topical lidocaine (2% gel, 5% ointment)         | Pain of fissure or haemorrhoids; before examination                                           | Thin layer; short courses; do not exceed product maximum                                                                                                                                             | Contact dermatitis; systemic toxicity with large mucosal doses                         |
| Topical diltiazem 2% / nifedipine 0.2–0.3%      | Chronic fissure (preferred first-line)                                                        | Pea-sized amount to the anal margin twice daily (some protocols three times) for 6–8 weeks                                                                                                           | Local itch or burning; headache uncommon                                               |
| Topical GTN 0.4%                                | Chronic fissure                                                                               | About 2.5 cm of ointment (≈ 1.5 mg GTN) every 12 hours for up to 8 weeks                                                                                                                             | Headache (common), dizziness, hypotension                                              |
| Topical corticosteroids                         | Short-term itch and oedema                                                                    | Low-potency (for example hydrocortisone 1%), thin layer, **≤ 2 weeks**                                                                                                                               | Atrophy, dermatitis, candida, masking of cancer                                        |
| Oral flavonoids (MPFF, diosmin–hesperidin)      | Acute haemorrhoidal symptoms (where licensed)                                                 | Per local labelling (commonly 3 g/day for 4 days, then 2 g/day for 3 days) [L]                                                                                                                       | GI upset, headache                                                                     |
| Antibiotics for anorectal sepsis                | Adjunct after drainage (cellulitis, systemic signs, immunosuppression); necrotising infection | Per local antibiogram [L]: e.g. amoxicillin–clavulanate 625 mg three times daily, or ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily + metronidazole 400–500 mg three times daily; severe sepsis: Table 5.3         | _C. difficile_, rash; QT prolongation and tendinopathy (quinolones)                    |
| Anticoagulants and antiplatelets                | Around banding, excision, and surgery                                                         | Plan with the prescriber [L]: warfarin often stopped ~5 days (INR < 1.5); DOACs 1–3 days by drug, renal function, and bleeding risk; P2Y12 inhibitors 5–7 days if stoppable; aspirin often continued | Delayed bleeding (days 5–10) after banding; thrombosis if stopped inappropriately      |
| Loperamide                                      | Loose stool; faecal incontinence                                                              | Incontinence: 2 mg, titrated; acute diarrhoea: 4 mg then 2 mg after each loose stool (max 16 mg/day)                                                                                                 | Constipation, ileus; QT prolongation and arrhythmia in overdose                        |
| SGLT2 inhibitors                                | Diabetes, heart failure, CKD                                                                  | —                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Genital infections; Fournier’s gangrene (rare, FDA 2018); euglycaemic DKA              |

**Table B.2 — Interactions, special populations, and when to avoid**

| Medicine                       | Major interactions                                                                    | Pregnancy / breastfeeding                       | Renal / hepatic                                                                         | Avoid when                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Psyllium                       | May reduce absorption of other drugs — separate by about 2 h                          | Suitable                                        | Caution in fluid restriction                                                            | Impaction, obstruction, dysphagia, poor fluid intake                                                                                                                       |
| Osmotic laxatives              | Magnesium chelates quinolones and tetracyclines                                       | PEG and lactulose suitable                      | Avoid magnesium in CKD                                                                  | Obstruction                                                                                                                                                                |
| Stimulant laxatives            | Additive hypokalaemia with diuretics or steroids                                      | Short-term use                                  | —                                                                                       | Obstruction; acute abdomen                                                                                                                                                 |
| Enemas and suppositories       | Phosphate + ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, NSAIDs                                   | Avoid phosphate enemas                          | Avoid phosphate in CKD                                                                  | Neutropenia, severe thrombocytopenia, suspected perforation or obstruction; arachis oil in peanut allergy (Appendix C)                                                     |
| Topical lidocaine              | Additive with other local anaesthetics or class I antiarrhythmics                     | Suitable short-term                             | Caution with large doses in liver disease                                               | Allergy; prolonged use                                                                                                                                                     |
| Topical diltiazem / nifedipine | Minimal systemic effect                                                               | Limited data — usually defer                    | —                                                                                       | Allergy                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Topical GTN                    | **PDE5 inhibitors (24–48 h), riociguat — contraindicated**                            | Limited data — generally avoid                  | —                                                                                       | Severe aortic or mitral stenosis, HOCM, raised intracranial pressure, hypotension                                                                                          |
| Topical corticosteroids        | —                                                                                     | Short-term low potency acceptable               | —                                                                                       | Infection, undiagnosed ulcer or plaque, > 2 weeks                                                                                                                          |
| Oral flavonoids                | Few known                                                                             | Limited data                                    | —                                                                                       | As a substitute for colon evaluation                                                                                                                                       |
| Antibiotics                    | Warfarin effect ↑ (metronidazole, ciprofloxacin); quinolone chelation with Mg, Ca, Fe | Avoid quinolones and tetracyclines in pregnancy | Renal dose adjustment (amoxicillin–clavulanate, ciprofloxacin, carbapenems, vancomycin) | As a substitute for drainage                                                                                                                                               |
| Anticoagulants / antiplatelets | NSAIDs, SSRIs increase bleeding                                                       | Specialist advice                               | DOAC accumulation in renal impairment (especially dabigatran)                           | Stopping after recent stents or with mechanical valves without cardiology input                                                                                            |
| Loperamide                     | QT-prolonging drugs; CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors raise levels                              | Occasional use                                  | Caution in hepatic impairment                                                           | Overflow or impaction, _C. difficile_, dysentery, bloody diarrhoea, fever, colitis, children with bloody diarrhoea                                                         |
| SGLT2 inhibitors               | —                                                                                     | Avoid in pregnancy                              | Per indication and eGFR                                                                 | **Stop immediately** if perineal infection is suspected; stop 3 days (ertugliflozin 4 days) before elective surgery per labelling; withhold when acutely unwell or fasting |
