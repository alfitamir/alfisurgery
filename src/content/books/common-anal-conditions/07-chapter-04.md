# Chapter 04 — Anal Fissure — Break the Pain–Spasm–Ischaemia Cycle

_Purpose: diagnose fissure by gentle inspection, relax the internal sphincter medically for 6–8 weeks, protect continence when medicine fails — and never miss the atypical fissure._

## 1. Recognise It on Gentle Inspection

A classic fissure is diagnosed by gently parting the buttocks while the patient bears down slightly. A DRE or anoscopy is **not** required for the diagnosis and is often intolerable in the acute phase.

**Table 4.1 — Acute versus chronic fissure**

| Feature         | Acute fissure (< 6 weeks)                                                              | Chronic fissure (≥ 6 weeks)                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Appearance      | Superficial linear tear with fresh, sharp edges                                        | Rolled, fibrotic edges; external **sentinel tag**; internal **hypertrophied papilla**; transverse internal-sphincter fibres visible at the base               |
| Pain            | Tearing pain during defecation (“passing broken glass”), bright-red blood on the paper | Pain during defecation followed by a deep ache lasting minutes to hours (sphincter spasm)                                                                     |
| Natural history | 🟢 Many heal with fibre, fluids, sitz baths, and topical analgesia                     | 🟡 Usually needs pharmacological sphincter relaxation for 6–8 weeks; if refractory → botulinum toxin or tailored lateral internal sphincterotomy (ASCRS 2023) |

### The location rule: midline is typical — anything else must be investigated

- **Typical:** posterior midline (the commonest site — the posterior commissure has the poorest anodermal blood supply); anterior midline (more frequent in women, especially postpartum).

- **Atypical:** lateral, multiple, painless, large or deep, irregular, indurated, or non-healing. Think **C-T-H-S-C**: **C**rohn’s disease · **T**uberculosis · **H**IV, herpes, CMV · **S**yphilis and chlamydia (LGV) · **C**ancer (anal squamous cell carcinoma, leukaemic or lymphomatous infiltration).

- **Action for an atypical fissure:** 🟡/🔴 urgent specialist referral for examination under anaesthesia with biopsy and cultures or PCR, colonoscopy where indicated, and a sexual-health screen. **Never perform a sphincterotomy on an atypical fissure.**

## 2. The 6–8 Week Medical Trial

Every chronic fissure persists through the same loop: hard stool → tear → pain → reflex internal-sphincter spasm → posterior midline ischaemia → non-healing. Healing requires softer stool **and** a relaxed internal sphincter (ASCRS 2023).

**Table 4.2 — Treatment options**

| Therapy                                                                                | How to use                                                                                                            | Safety profile                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Verdict (ASCRS 2023)                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Fibre, fluids, stool softener, warm sitz baths, topical lidocaine                      | Throughout treatment and afterwards                                                                                   | Very safe; lidocaine can cause contact dermatitis with prolonged use                                                                                                                                                    | 🟢 Foundation for every fissure [S]                                                                                                                                                  |
| Topical calcium-channel blockers: **diltiazem 2%** or **nifedipine 0.2–0.3%** ointment | Pea-sized amount (about 1–2 cm of ointment) to the anal margin twice daily (some protocols three times) for 6–8 weeks | Occasional local itching or burning; headache uncommon. Often compounded or off-label [L]                                                                                                                               | 🟢 **Preferred first-line** — healing comparable to nitrates with fewer side effects [S]                                                                                             |
| Topical **glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) 0.2–0.4%** ointment                                | Twice daily for 6–8 weeks (0.4% is licensed for this use in some countries)                                           | Headache is common and causes many patients to stop. **Contraindicated with PDE5 inhibitors and riociguat**; avoid in severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, and raised intracranial pressure | 🟡 Effective alternative first-line agent [S]                                                                                                                                        |
| **Botulinum toxin** injection into the internal sphincter                              | Single injection; dose and site are **not standardised** (a wide range of doses has been reported) [U]                | Temporary, usually minor incontinence to flatus that resolves as the effect wears off; recurrence possible                                                                                                              | 🟡 Reasonable second-line, or first-line alternative when continence risk is high                                                                                                    |
| **Tailored lateral internal sphincterotomy (LIS)**                                     | Division of the internal sphincter limited to the height of the fissure                                               | Small but real risk of minor, mainly flatus, incontinence — rates vary between series; permanent if excessive                                                                                                           | 🟡 **Most effective definitive treatment** for chronic fissure failing medical therapy; may be offered to selected patients without a medical trial after shared decision-making [S] |

<small>Uncontrolled manual anal stretch (Lord’s procedure) is obsolete because of unpredictable sphincter injury. Low resting pressure, previous obstetric sphincter injury, prior anal surgery, or IBD favour sphincter-sparing options (botulinum toxin or anocutaneous advancement flap).</small>

## 3. Dangerous Mimics

**Table 4.3 — Fissure mimics**

| Mimic                        | Clue                                                                                         | Action                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Anal squamous cell carcinoma | Indurated, heaped, or ulcerated edges; mass; inguinal nodes; non-healing                     | 🟡 Urgent biopsy (Chapter 07)                            |
| Perianal Crohn’s disease     | Lateral, multiple, deep, relatively painless ulcers; oedematous tags; diarrhoea, weight loss | 🟡 Gastroenterology and colorectal referral (Chapter 09) |
| Syphilitic chancre           | Indurated, often painless ulcer; may be multiple or “kissing”; inguinal nodes                | 🟡 Serology; sexual-health treatment (Chapter 11)        |
| Herpes simplex               | Multiple shallow, very painful ulcers or vesicles; systemic symptoms                         | 🟡 HSV PCR; antiviral therapy (Chapter 11)               |
| Intersphincteric abscess     | Fever, constant deep pain, tender bulge on DRE                                               | 🔴 Same-day surgical review (Chapter 05)                 |
| Tuberculosis                 | Chronic undermined ulcer; pulmonary or systemic TB features                                  | 🟡 Biopsy, culture, specialist referral                  |

## 4. Role-Targeted Action Boxes

> **🚑 ER DOCTOR ACTION BOX**
>
> - **Skip the traumatic DRE and anoscopy in an acute fissure.** If gentle inspection shows a fresh posterior midline tear and the patient is in severe pain, treat the spasm (topical lidocaine, topical calcium-channel blocker, stool softener, oral analgesia).
> - **Fever, induration, or a suspected mass changes everything** — this is not a simple fissure; refer the same day for examination under anaesthesia.

> **🩺 FAMILY MEDICINE / GP CLINIC BOX**
>
> - **Teach ointment use:** a pea-sized amount applied with a gloved fingertip to the anal margin twice daily for the full 6–8 weeks. _“The pain usually settles within 2 weeks, but the skin needs 6–8 weeks to heal — stopping early lets the tear reopen.”_
> - **Ask about PDE5 inhibitors (including “herbal” erection products, which may contain them) and riociguat before prescribing GTN.** Prefer diltiazem when in doubt.
> - **Review at 6–8 weeks.** Refer if not healed, or earlier if atypical.
> - **For a postpartum anterior fissure,** ask about continence and obstetric sphincter injury before any surgical referral.

> **🏥 ER & WARD NURSE CARE AND SBAR BOX**
>
> - **Document by anatomy, never by the clock.** Chart “posterior midline” or “anterior midline” — the same fissure acquires different clock numbers in lithotomy, prone, and left lateral positions.
> - **GTN counselling:** first doses may cause headache or light-headedness; apply while sitting or lying, and use simple analgesia for headache.

> **💊 CROSS-SPECIALTY PRESCRIBING TRAP**
>
> - **Topical GTN plus PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, vardenafil, tadalafil, avanafil) or riociguat** can cause profound hypotension and syncope. Do not use GTN within 24 hours of sildenafil or vardenafil, or 48 hours of tadalafil. **Topical diltiazem or nifedipine avoids this interaction.**

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never perform or request a sphincterotomy for an atypical fissure, or in active Crohn’s disease.
> - Never prescribe GTN without asking about PDE5 inhibitors and riociguat.
> - Never force a DRE or anoscope through acute fissure spasm.
> - Never request a manual anal stretch.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **Clock-face notation** (“fissure at 6 o’clock”) without position — use anterior or posterior midline, or left or right lateral.
> - **“Fissure”** without acute or chronic features (tag, papilla, exposed fibres) and without stating whether it is typical or atypical.
> - **Surgical referral without baseline continence, obstetric history, and previous anal surgery.**

**📨 REFERRAL URGENCY BOX**

| Urgency     | Clinical trigger                                                                                                       | Who and how fast                                                      |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 SAME-DAY | Fissure-type pain with fever, induration, or a tender bulge (possible abscess)                                         | On-call surgeon; EUA                                                  |
| 🟡 URGENT   | Atypical fissure; fissure with a mass or induration; non-healing fissure in HIV, Crohn’s disease, or immunosuppression | Colorectal surgery ± gastroenterology / sexual health; biopsy         |
| 🟢 ROUTINE  | Typical chronic fissure not healed after 6–8 weeks of medical therapy                                                  | Colorectal or general surgical clinic (botulinum toxin, LIS, or flap) |

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 04 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> A 29-year-old man presents with 4 weeks of “razor-blade” pain on defecation. Gentle inspection shows two ragged, relatively painless ulcers on the left and right lateral walls of the anal canal, with oedematous, violaceous skin tags. He reports 6 months of intermittent loose stools and 4 kg of unintentional weight loss.

**Step 1** **Spot the red-flag location.** Lateral, multiple, relatively painless ulcers with oedematous tags are atypical — not idiopathic fissures.

**Step 2** **Build the differential (C-T-H-S-C).** Perianal Crohn’s disease is most likely; also consider syphilis, herpes, HIV, TB, and anal cancer.

**Step 3** **Investigate.** Faecal calprotectin, full blood count, CRP, ferritin, stool studies, HIV and syphilis serology, and rectal NAAT/HSV PCR as indicated.

**Step 4** **Refer and protect.** Urgent gastroenterology and colorectal referral for ileocolonoscopy, pelvic MRI if fistulising disease is suspected, and examination under anaesthesia with biopsy. **No sphincterotomy.**

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 04

**A 34-year-old woman (three vaginal deliveries, a third-degree obstetric tear 4 years ago, occasional flatus incontinence) has a chronic anterior midline fissure that has not healed after 8 weeks of topical diltiazem 2% and fibre. Resting tone is normal to low. Which next step best balances healing and continence?**

- **A.** Lateral internal sphincterotomy extended to the dentate line
- **B.** Botulinum toxin injection (or an anocutaneous advancement flap)
- **C.** Manual four-finger anal stretch under general anaesthesia
- **D.** Repeat topical GTN 0.4% combined with oral sildenafil

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Extended sphincterotomy in a patient with prior sphincter injury and existing flatus incontinence risks permanent incontinence; even when LIS is appropriate it is tailored to the fissure apex.
- ✅ **B** **Correct.** With prior obstetric sphincter injury, an anterior fissure, existing incontinence symptoms, and low-normal tone, sphincter-sparing options — botulinum toxin or an anocutaneous advancement flap — are preferred (ASCRS 2023).
- ❌ **C** Manual anal stretch causes uncontrolled sphincter tearing and is obsolete.
- ❌ **D** Nitrates and PDE5 inhibitors must never be combined because of severe hypotension.

## Ten-Item Rapid Revision Checklist — Chapter 04

- [ ] 01 I diagnose fissure by gentle inspection without forcing a DRE.
- [ ] 02 I distinguish acute (< 6 weeks) from chronic (sentinel tag, papilla, exposed fibres).
- [ ] 03 I recognise atypical fissures and apply C-T-H-S-C.
- [ ] 04 I start fibre, fluids, sitz baths, and topical lidocaine for every fissure.
- [ ] 05 I use topical diltiazem or nifedipine as preferred first-line therapy for 6–8 weeks.
- [ ] 06 I ask about PDE5 inhibitors and riociguat before prescribing GTN.
- [ ] 07 I teach correct ointment application and duration.
- [ ] 08 I review at 6–8 weeks and refer non-healing fissures.
- [ ] 09 I document continence and obstetric history in every surgical referral.
- [ ] 10 I record location anatomically, never by clock face.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 04

1. ⚠️ Referring an atypical fissure for sphincterotomy and missing Crohn’s disease, infection, or cancer.
2. ⚠️ Combining GTN with a PDE5 inhibitor.
3. ⚠️ Stopping topical therapy after 2 weeks because pain has improved.
4. ⚠️ Recommending sphincterotomy in a woman with prior obstetric sphincter injury without considering sphincter-sparing options.
5. ⚠️ Calling an indurated, non-healing anal ulcer a “chronic fissure” for months.
