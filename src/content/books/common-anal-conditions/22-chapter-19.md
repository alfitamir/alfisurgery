## 1. Master Comparison Table

**Table 19.1 — Ten core conditions**

| Condition | Hallmark | Key finding | First-line management | Escalation / pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Internal haemorrhoids | Painless bright-red bleeding ± prolapse | Seen on anoscopy, not felt on DRE | 🟢 Fibre 25–35 g/day total, toilet habits; RBL for refractory I–III (IRC in significant coagulopathy) | 🔴 Never band R2/T1; pelvic sepsis triad; colon first if red flags |

## 2. Ten Rapid-Fire Bedside Rules

1. Most resting anal pressure comes from one involuntary muscle — the internal sphincter. Protect it.
2. Abscess → drain. Thrombosed haemorrhoid → remove the whole clot. Never confuse them.
3. Concentric rings = rectal prolapse; radial grooves = haemorrhoids. Check the colour before you touch.
4. Fibre (25–35 g/day total) roughly halves persisting symptoms and bleeding — and the phone stays outside the bathroom.
5. Worsening pain + fever + urinary difficulty after banding = pelvic sepsis until proven otherwise.
6. Give chronic fissures a full 6–8-week medical trial — topical diltiazem or nifedipine first; never GTN with a PDE5 inhibitor.
7. Lateral, multiple, or painless fissures are not idiopathic — think C-T-H-S-C and biopsy.
8. No routine packing after abscess drainage (PPAC2), and never wait for fluctuance.
9. Document by anatomical quadrant, never clock face — and no routine rectal instrumentation in profound neutropenia.
10. Plain warm-water sitz baths; escalate by SBAR for temperature above 38.0 °C, no void in 6–8 h, bleeding > 1 pad/h, or escalating pain.

**Algorithm 19.1 — The 03:00 one-page triage**

```text
[ANY ANORECTAL PATIENT AT 03:00]
|
+-- Sick? (sepsis, shock index > 1, crepitus, peritonism,
|   post-procedure triad, cauda equina) -> RED: surgeon now
+-- Pus? -> drain today (no fluctuance needed)
+-- Clot < 72 h? -> remove the whole clot (or conservative)
+-- Prolapse? -> colour, then rings vs grooves
+-- Bleeding? -> shock index, then alarm features (Ch 08)
+-- Ulcer/mass/plaque not healing? -> biopsy (Ch 07)
+-- Discharge + tenesmus? -> STI tests + treat (Ch 11)
+-- Atypical fissure/recurrent abscess? -> Crohn's (Ch 09)
+-- Leakage? -> DRE before loperamide (Ch 06, 10)
+-- Otherwise -> GREEN pathway + dated review
```

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never let the label “piles” replace an examination.
> - Never wait for fluctuance, crepitus, or a scan to call surgery.
> - Never divide a sphincter without documenting continence and excluding Crohn’s disease.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **The single most common error:** a diagnosis without a documented examination.

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 19 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> A 45-year-old man has 3 days of worsening throbbing perianal pain. He cannot sit or open his bowels. Temperature 38.5 °C, heart rate 102/min. In the left lateral position there is a red, warm, exquisitely tender indurated area at the left lateral anal margin, with no fluctuance.

**Step 1** **Diagnose:** fever + progressive throbbing pain + localised induration = perianal or ischioanal abscess, even without fluctuance.

**Step 2** **No MRI first:** for a clear primary abscess, imaging only delays source control (ASCRS 2022).

**Step 3** **Act:** same-day incision and drainage close to the verge; no routine packing (PPAC2); voiding check, multimodal analgesia, warm-water sitz baths; warn him that a fistula may follow and arrange follow-up.

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 19

**A ward nurse calls about a man 8 hours after haemorrhoidectomy who is soaking more than one pad per hour (HR 112/min, BP 102/64 mmHg). Which SBAR component is the statement: “Please review at the bedside within 15 minutes and confirm if you want FBC, group-and-crossmatch, and theatre on standby”?**

- **A.** Situation
- **B.** Background
- **C.** Assessment
- **D.** Recommendation

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Situation identifies the caller, patient, and headline problem.
- ❌ **B** Background gives clinical context (operation, pre-operative Hb, anticoagulants).
- ❌ **C** Assessment summarises current observations and findings.
- ✅ **D** **Correct.** Recommendation states the action required and its time frame.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 19

1. ⚠️ Waiting for fluctuance before draining an abscess.
2. ⚠️ Discharging the pelvic sepsis triad as “post-banding discomfort”.
3. ⚠️ Treating bleeding “piles” without colon evaluation in a patient with red flags.
4. ⚠️ Missing STI proctitis or Crohn’s disease behind an atypical lesion.
5. ⚠️ Escalating without a clear, time-bound request.
