# Chapter 13 — Pregnancy, Postpartum, and Pelvic-Floor Anorectal Disease

_Purpose: pregnancy multiplies anorectal symptoms; most respond to simple, safe measures — know which drugs and procedures to avoid, and never miss sphincter injury after childbirth._

## 1. Common Problems in Pregnancy and After Delivery

**Table 13.1 — Problems and first-line care**

| Problem                             | Features                                                                                       | First-line care                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Constipation                        | Progesterone, iron, reduced activity, pressure from the uterus                                 | Fibre, fluids, activity; lactulose or macrogol (Table 13.2)                                                              |
| Haemorrhoids                        | Up to two-thirds of pregnant women (AGA 2026); worse in the third trimester and after delivery | Conservative: fibre, treating constipation, topical agents; banding or IRC after delivery if still needed (AGA 2026) [C] |
| Thrombosed external haemorrhoid     | Late pregnancy or soon after delivery; sudden painful bluish lump                              | Conservative; excision under local anaesthetic is acceptable if pain is severe and onset under 72 hours [C]              |
| Anal fissure                        | Often anterior and postpartum                                                                  | Fibre, softeners, warm sitz baths, topical lidocaine; defer other agents (Table 13.2)                                    |
| Pruritus and perianal dermatitis    | Candida is common in pregnancy                                                                 | Hygiene measures, barrier, topical azole                                                                                 |
| Perianal abscess                    | Same features as outside pregnancy                                                             | **Drain promptly** — pregnancy is not a reason to delay; involve obstetrics                                              |
| Rectal bleeding with alarm features | Persistent bleeding, anaemia, weight loss, diarrhoea                                           | Investigate as in Chapter 08 — IBD and cancer can occur in pregnancy                                                     |

## 3. Procedures in Pregnancy

**Table 13.3 — What is safe, and when**

| Procedure                                         | Guidance [C]                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Incision and drainage of abscess                  | Do it promptly, as for non-pregnant patients; local or regional anaesthesia; inform obstetrics                                            |
| Excision of acute thrombosed external haemorrhoid | Acceptable under local anaesthetic if severe pain and onset under 72 hours                                                                |
| Banding, IRC, sclerotherapy                       | Defer until after delivery unless bleeding is severe (AGA 2026)                                                                           |
| Haemorrhoidectomy                                 | Only for complications (strangulation, uncontrolled bleeding), with obstetric input; the second trimester is preferred when timing allows |
| Sphincterotomy, botulinum toxin                   | Defer until after delivery (and discuss during breastfeeding)                                                                             |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy or colonoscopy             | Only for strong indications (for example significant bleeding with alarm features); specialist and obstetric planning                     |
| Positioning                                       | Left lateral or left-tilted after 20 weeks to avoid supine hypotension                                                                    |

## 4. Postpartum Constipation, OASIS, and Faecal Incontinence

- **After any perineal repair — especially OASIS — prescribe laxatives and give analgesia** to avoid straining (RCOG Green-top 29) [C]. Avoid codeine and tramadol while breastfeeding.

- **Ask about continence at every postnatal check** (gas, liquid, solid, urgency). New symptoms need pelvic-floor physiotherapy, perineal or colorectal clinic review, and endoanal ultrasound where available.

- **Stool or wind passing from the vagina** suggests a rectovaginal fistula — urgent specialist referral.

- **Future deliveries:** women with previous OASIS should be counselled about mode of delivery; persistent symptoms or abnormal anal physiology may favour caesarean section (RCOG Green-top 29) [C].

```text
[PREGNANT OR POSTPARTUM PATIENT WITH ANORECTAL SYMPTOMS]

```

## 5. Dangerous Mimics

**Table 13.4 — Pregnancy and postpartum mimics**

| Presumed                                    | Actual                                   | Clue                                        |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Thrombosed haemorrhoid                      | Perianal abscess                         | Fever, induration, gradual onset            |
| Postpartum “leakage”                        | Rectovaginal fistula                     | Stool or wind from the vagina               |
| Postpartum “piles”                          | Full-thickness prolapse                  | Concentric folds                            |
| Painful “fissure”                           | Herpes                                   | Multiple ulcers or vesicles                 |
| Pregnancy constipation                      | Bowel obstruction (for example volvulus) | Vomiting, distension, absolute constipation |
| Bleeding “from piles” persisting postpartum | IBD or, rarely, cancer                   | Diarrhoea, anaemia, weight loss             |

## 6. Role-Targeted Action Boxes

> **🚑 ER DOCTOR ACTION BOX**
>
> - **Pregnancy never delays abscess drainage.** Involve obstetrics, position with left tilt, and use local or regional anaesthesia.
> - **Severe pain, early thrombosed external haemorrhoid:** excision under local anaesthetic is acceptable.

> **🩺 FAMILY MEDICINE / GP CLINIC BOX**
>
> - **Start with constipation:** fibre, fluids, lactulose or macrogol.
> - **Ask about continence at the 6-week check** and refer early after OASIS.
> - **Check every prescription against pregnancy and breastfeeding guidance** (Table 13.2).

> **🏥 ER & WARD NURSE CARE AND SBAR BOX**
>
> - **Postnatal teach-back:** laxatives, perineal hygiene, warm sitz baths, and the warning signs (fever above 38.0 °C, wound breakdown, foul discharge, leakage of stool).

> **💊 CROSS-SPECIALTY PRESCRIBING TRAP**
>
> - **NSAIDs from 20 weeks** (FDA 2020) and **codeine or tramadol while breastfeeding** (FDA 2017) are the commonest prescribing traps.
> - **Doxycycline, ciprofloxacin, and castor oil** should not be used in pregnancy.

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never delay drainage of a perianal abscess because a patient is pregnant.
> - Never band haemorrhoids routinely during pregnancy.
> - Never prescribe codeine or tramadol to a breastfeeding mother.
> - Never dismiss postpartum incontinence as normal.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **Gestation, breastfeeding status, and obstetric team involvement** not recorded.
> - **Postnatal notes without a continence enquiry** after a perineal tear.

**📨 REFERRAL URGENCY BOX**

| Urgency      | Clinical trigger                                                                                  | Who and how fast                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 EMERGENCY | Sepsis, heavy bleeding with instability, suspected bowel obstruction                              | Surgeon and obstetrician now                        |
| 🔴 SAME-DAY  | Perianal abscess; severe early thrombosed haemorrhoid; strangulated haemorrhoids                  | Surgical review with obstetric input                |
| 🟡 URGENT    | Postpartum incontinence after OASIS; suspected rectovaginal fistula; bleeding with alarm features | Perineal, colorectal, or urogynaecology clinic      |
| 🟢 ROUTINE   | Persistent haemorrhoids after delivery                                                            | Office-procedure clinic after the postpartum period |

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 13 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> A 31-year-old woman at 34 weeks’ gestation presents with a 30-hour history of an exquisitely painful bluish lump at the anal verge. She is afebrile and constipated. Fetal movements are normal.

**Step 1** **Diagnose:** acute thrombosed external haemorrhoid within 72 hours; no features of abscess.

**Step 2** **Choose with her:** excision under local anaesthetic (left-tilted position) gives faster relief and is acceptable in pregnancy; conservative care is also reasonable [C].

**Step 3** **Treat the cause:** lactulose or macrogol, fibre, fluids; paracetamol (no NSAIDs at 34 weeks).

**Step 4** **Inform obstetrics** and safety-net for bleeding, fever, or contractions.

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 13

**A breastfeeding mother needs analgesia after repair of a third-degree perineal tear. Which regimen is most appropriate?**

- **A.** Codeine 30–60 mg four times daily
- **B.** Regular paracetamol plus ibuprofen, with a laxative
- **C.** Tramadol 50 mg four times daily
- **D.** No analgesia, to avoid any drug exposure for the baby

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Codeine is not recommended in breastfeeding because ultra-rapid metabolisers can expose the infant to toxic morphine levels (FDA 2017).
- ✅ **B** **Correct.** Paracetamol and ibuprofen are compatible with breastfeeding, and laxatives prevent straining on the repair (RCOG Green-top 29).
- ❌ **C** Tramadol carries the same breastfeeding warning as codeine (FDA 2017).
- ❌ **D** Untreated pain impairs mobility, voiding, and breastfeeding.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 13

1. ⚠️ Delaying abscess drainage because of pregnancy.
2. ⚠️ Prescribing an NSAID in the third trimester.
3. ⚠️ Prescribing codeine to a breastfeeding mother.

5. ⚠️ Using a sodium phosphate enema in pregnancy.
