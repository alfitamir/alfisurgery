## 1. The Five-Step Systematic Examination

**Table 17.1 — Five steps**

| Step                         | Technique                                                                                                                                                                            | Looking or feeling for                                                                                              | Pearl                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01 Prepare                   | Explain each step; obtain consent; chaperone; left lateral (Sims) position with hips flexed, at the edge of the couch, under good light                                              | A relaxed, informed patient                                                                                         | 🟢 Check Appendix C first (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, sharp foreign body, child abuse concerns) |
| 02 Inspect (rest and strain) | Part the buttocks gently; ask the patient to bear down                                                                                                                               | Tags, thrombosis, fissure, fistula openings, pilonidal pits, dermatitis, warts, ulcers, prolapse (rings or grooves) | 🟢 Most fissures and external thromboses are diagnosed here, without inserting a finger             |
| 03 Palpate (DRE)             | Generous lubricant (lidocaine gel if tender, waiting 5–10 min); rest the finger pad on the verge, then enter slowly as the patient bears down; sweep 360°; assess prostate or cervix | Resting tone, squeeze, push manoeuvre, masses, induration, deep tenderness, stool, blood, mucus, pus on the glove   | 🔴 Severe pain or spasm → stop; consider EUA                                                        |
| 04 Anoscopy                  | Lubricated anoscope with obturator; withdraw the obturator; inspect the cushions and dentate line while the patient strains gently; remove before rotating                           | Internal haemorrhoids (not palpable), crypts, internal openings, proctitis, low polyps or tumours                   | 🟡 Every new “haemorrhoid” patient where available (AGA 2026)                                       |
| 05 Document                  | Position, chaperone, findings by anatomical quadrant and distance from the verge                                                                                                     | Anterior, posterior, left lateral, right lateral (± anterolateral, posterolateral)                                  | 🔴 Never use clock-face numbers alone                                                               |

## 2. When to Stop and Book an Examination Under Anaesthesia

1. Severe spasm or intolerable pain despite topical lidocaine with an adequate dwell time.
2. Suspected deep intersphincteric or supralevator abscess (fever, deep rectal pain, normal skin).
3. Atypical, ulcerated, or complex disease needing biopsy, probing, seton placement, or drainage.
4. Children or patients unable to cooperate, when examination is essential.

## 3. Why Clock Faces Fail

**Table 17.2 — The same posterior midline lesion in different positions**

| Position                        | Common clock description                        | Problem                                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lithotomy (supine, legs raised) | 6 o’clock                                       | Assumes the examiner faces the perineum with 12 o’clock anterior                 |
| Prone jack-knife                | Often 12 o’clock                                | Many surgeons place 12 o’clock at the sacrum in this position — conventions vary |
| Left lateral (Sims)             | Variable (3 or 9 o’clock, or 6 o’clock by some) | No universal convention                                                          |
| **Any position**                | **“Posterior midline, 1 cm from the verge”**    | **Unambiguous** — use anatomical terms                                           |

**Algorithm 17.1 — The tolerable anorectal examination**

```text
[PATIENT NEEDS AN ANORECTAL EXAMINATION]
|
+-- CHECK APPENDIX C FIRST: neutropenia, severe
|   thrombocytopenia, sharp foreign body, child-abuse
|   concern -> visual inspection only / specialist route
|
+-- Consent + chaperone + left lateral + good light
|   -> inspect at rest and on straining
|
+-- Severe pain or spasm, OR deep sepsis suspected?
|     +-- typical midline fissure, afebrile
|     |     -> treat the fissure; defer DRE/anoscopy
|     +-- fever, atypical ulcer, mass, or abscess
|           -> stop; urgent EUA
|
+-- Tolerable -> lidocaine gel (5-10 min dwell)
    -> DRE: rest tone, squeeze, push, masses, glove
    -> anoscopy: cushions, dentate line, mucosa
    -> document by ANATOMICAL quadrant + distance
```

## 4. Examination Traps

**Table 17.3 — What a normal-looking examination can miss**

| Trap                            | Why                                                          | Remedy                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| “DRE negative for haemorrhoids” | Internal haemorrhoids are soft and collapse under the finger | Anoscopy                                        |
| Normal skin, febrile patient    | Intersphincteric or supralevator abscess                     | Feel for a tender bulge; EUA or imaging         |
| No prolapse on the couch        | Prolapse may appear only on straining on the toilet          | Examine seated on a commode or straining        |
| Fissure spasm prevents DRE      | A low rectal tumour can hide behind it                       | Treat spasm and re-examine in 2–3 weeks, or EUA |
| Groins not examined             | Inguinal nodes signal anal cancer or infection               | Examine both groins                             |

## 5. Role-Targeted Action Boxes

> **🚑 ER DOCTOR ACTION BOX**
>
> - **Internal haemorrhoids are invisible to your finger.** Never write “DRE negative for haemorrhoids” — anoscopy shows haemorrhoids; DRE finds masses, tone, and deep abscesses.

> **🩺 FAMILY MEDICINE / GP CLINIC BOX**
>
> - **Give lidocaine gel time to work:** apply to the verge, finish the history (5–10 minutes), then examine.

> **🏥 ER & WARD NURSE CARE AND SBAR BOX**
>
> - **Positioning makes or breaks the examination:** help the patient to the edge of the couch in left lateral with hips flexed and knees drawn up.

> **💊 CROSS-SPECIALTY PRESCRIBING TRAP**
>
> - **An incomplete examination is not a reason for empirical cream without follow-up.** Book EUA if red flags exist, or a re-examination in 2–3 weeks once spasm is treated.

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never force an examination or restrain a patient for it.
> - Never document by clock face alone.
> - Never examine without a chaperone offered.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **Missing:** position, chaperone, consent, resting tone versus squeeze, and distance from the verge.

**📨 REFERRAL URGENCY BOX**

| Urgency     | Clinical trigger                                          | Who and how fast            |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 🔴 SAME-DAY | Examination impossible with fever or suspected abscess    | Urgent EUA                  |
| 🟡 URGENT   | Mass, ulcer, induration, or nodes found                   | Biopsy pathway (Chapter 07) |
| 🟢 ROUTINE  | Incomplete examination due to fissure spasm, no red flags | Re-examine in 2–3 weeks     |

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 17 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> A 41-year-old man is listed for “excision of a thrombosed external nodule and fistulotomy at 3 o’clock”. The clinic note does not state the examination position or convention. In theatre he is placed prone jack-knife, and the resident prepares to operate on the right lateral side.

**Step 1** **Recognise the ambiguity:** “3 o’clock” means different walls in different positions and conventions — the note cannot be interpreted safely.

**Step 2** **Verify anatomically:** pause, examine the full circumference under anaesthesia, and confirm the actual lesion and tract before incising.

**Step 3** **Fix the system:** adopt anatomical-quadrant documentation (anterior, posterior, left lateral, right lateral, plus distance from the verge) in all clinic, ER, and operative notes.

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 17

**A 37-year-old man has had 10 days of severe anal pain and a temperature of 38.1 °C. Despite lidocaine gel for 10 minutes, he cannot tolerate a touch at the verge. What is the best next step?**

- **A.** Have assistants restrain him and complete the DRE
- **B.** Document “non-compliant” and discharge on oral ciprofloxacin
- **C.** Stop and arrange an urgent examination under anaesthesia
- **D.** Routine review in 6 weeks

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Forcing the examination is unethical, traumatic, and unreliable.
- ❌ **B** Antibiotics without diagnosis mask an undrained abscess; “non-compliant” is a documentation error.
- ✅ **C** **Correct.** Fever with intolerable pain suggests an intersphincteric or deep abscess; EUA allows diagnosis and drainage in one sitting.
- ❌ **D** A 6-week delay is dangerous in a febrile patient.

## Ten-Item Rapid Revision Checklist — Chapter 17

- [ ] 01 I check Appendix C before any rectal examination.
- [ ] 02 I obtain consent and offer a chaperone.
- [ ] 03 I inspect at rest and on straining before inserting a finger.
- [ ] 04 I allow 5–10 minutes for lidocaine gel to work.
- [ ] 05 I record resting tone, squeeze, and push separately.
- [ ] 06 I use anoscopy to see internal haemorrhoids.
- [ ] 07 I stop and book EUA when pain prevents a safe examination.
- [ ] 08 I examine both groins when a lesion is found.
- [ ] 09 I document by anatomical quadrant and distance from the verge.
- [ ] 10 I arrange re-examination when the first examination was incomplete.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 17

1. ⚠️ Forcing a DRE in acute fissure or abscess.
2. ⚠️ Writing “no haemorrhoids” after a DRE alone.
3. ⚠️ Using clock-face notation without position and convention.
4. ⚠️ Missing a deep abscess because the skin looked normal.
5. ⚠️ Treating empirically after an incomplete examination with no follow-up.
