## 1. The Three-Phase Perioperative Nursing Journey

**Table 18.1 — Nursing checklist by phase**

| Phase | Checklist | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Before surgery | • Consent, identity, and site verified<br>• Fasting and any bowel preparation<br>• Antibiotic prophylaxis if prescribed<br>• Antithrombotic hold confirmed<br>• **Void immediately before theatre** | A full bladder plus anaesthesia and perineal pain is the classic route to post-operative retention |
| On the ward | • Pain scores; scheduled multimodal analgesia; minimal opioid<br>• Encourage oral fluids; avoid excessive IV fluids once drinking<br>• Voiding within 6–8 hours; bladder scan if not<br>• Check dressing and pad count regularly in the first hours<br>• Laxative and fibre from day 1 | Prevents retention, hidden haemorrhage, and a feared, painful first bowel movement |
| Going home | • Fibre, fluids, and stool softener<br>• Plain warm-water sitz baths<br>• Written return triggers (Appendix D)<br>• **Teach-back** before the cannula is removed | Patients in pain forget verbal advice; teach-back confirms understanding |

## 2. The Plain Warm-Water Sitz Bath Rule

- **Plain warm water (about 37–40 °C) for 10–15 minutes, 2–3 times daily and after bowel movements** — soothing and cleansing without rubbing [C]. Data for sitz baths are limited (AGA 2026) [U].

- **Additives are unnecessary.** Salt has no proven benefit.

- **Avoid antiseptics** — chloroxylenol (Dettol), cetrimide, povidone-iodine, or potassium permanganate concentrates — which irritate or burn anoderm and open wounds [C].

## 3. SBAR Escalation Thresholds

**Table 18.2 — Four numerical triggers**

| Parameter | Escalate when | Do before or during the call | What the team will consider |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌡️ Fever | **Temperature above 38.0 °C**, or below 36.0 °C with rigors or tachycardia | Full observations; inspect perineum for spreading redness, crepitus, pus | Pelvic sepsis, undrained abscess, early Fournier’s gangrene |
| 💧 Urinary retention | No void within 6–8 hours, or bladder scan > 400 mL with discomfort | Help to stand or use a warm shower; bladder scan; report volume | Catheterisation; review opioids |
| 🩸 Bleeding | > 1 pad per hour, clots, heart rate > 110/min, or systolic BP < 100 mmHg | Direct pressure; observations; check IV access; hold antithrombotics | Reactionary or secondary haemorrhage; tamponade or theatre |
| ⚡ Pain | ≥ 7/10 or escalating despite scheduled analgesia | Bladder scan first; inspect for haematoma or swelling | Retention, haematoma, misplaced band, sepsis |

> **📞 READY-TO-USE SBAR SCRIPT**
>
> - **S:** “Dr [name], I am calling from ward [X] about [patient], bed [Y], 6 hours after excisional haemorrhoidectomy, soaking more than one pad per hour with bright blood and clots.”
> - **B:** “Three-column haemorrhoidectomy at 10:00. Pre-operative Hb 134 g/L. No anticoagulants.”
> - **A:** “Three pads in 2 hours; heart rate up from 78 to 108/min; BP 104/66 mmHg; temperature 37.1 °C. I am applying pressure; IV access is working.”
> - **R:** “Please review at the bedside within 15–30 minutes. Shall I send FBC, coagulation, and group-and-crossmatch now?”

**Algorithm 18.1 — Post-operative ward escalation**

```text
[POST-ANORECTAL PROCEDURE MONITORING]
|
+-- Temperature above 38.0 C -> inspect perineum for
|   redness, crepitus, pus -> SBAR (pelvic sepsis?)
|
+-- No void in 6-8 h -> warm shower, bladder scan
|   -> > 400 mL with discomfort -> SBAR (catheter)
|
+-- Bleeding > 1 pad/h, clots, HR > 110, SBP < 100
|   -> pressure, observations, IV access -> SBAR
|
+-- Pain >= 7/10 and rising -> BLADDER SCAN FIRST,
|   inspect for haematoma -> SBAR
|
+-- Every SBAR call ends with a time-bound request:
    "Please review within 15-30 minutes"
```

## 5. Ward Traps

| Trap | Reality |
|---|---|
| “Anal pain” and restlessness after surgery | Often urinary retention — scan the bladder |
| Small stain on the pad | Bleeding can pool under the patient or in the rectum — check observations and the bed |
| Tachycardia with little visible blood | Concealed haemorrhage or sepsis |
| Day-3 fever after banding | Pelvic sepsis until proven otherwise |

## 6. Role-Targeted Action Boxes

> **🚑 ER DOCTOR ACTION BOX**
>
> - **Recurrent rectal prolapse in a child** → think cystic fibrosis and refer (Chapter 14).

> **🩺 FAMILY MEDICINE / GP CLINIC BOX**
>
> - **Pregnant women need fibre and reassurance first,** not steroid marathons; most haemorrhoids improve after delivery (Chapter 13).

> **🏥 ER & WARD NURSE CARE AND SBAR BOX**
>
> - **End every SBAR call with a time-bound recommendation,** never “just letting you know”.

> **💊 CROSS-SPECIALTY PRESCRIBING TRAP**
>
> - **Codeine or tramadol as the main discharge analgesic** after haemorrhoidectomy produces hard stools on day 3–4. Prefer regular paracetamol plus an NSAID (if suitable), topical agents, and a daily stool softener.

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never add antiseptics to a sitz bath.
> - Never give more opioid for post-operative pain before scanning the bladder.
> - Never hand over “monitor closely” without numbers.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **Pad counts, void times, and bladder-scan volumes** not recorded; SBAR calls without the time and the response.

**📨 REFERRAL URGENCY BOX**

| Urgency | Clinical trigger | Who and how fast |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 EMERGENCY | Bleeding with instability; fever with spreading cellulitis or crepitus | Surgeon at bedside now |
| 🔴 SAME-DAY | Any SBAR threshold crossed | Surgical review within 15–30 minutes |
| 🟢 ROUTINE | Expected recovery | Discharge with Appendix D sheet |

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 18 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> Seven hours after a three-column haemorrhoidectomy under spinal anaesthesia, a 48-year-old man reports 8/10 lower abdominal and perineal pain and restlessness. He has not passed urine despite drinking. The pad shows a small serosanguinous stain. Bladder scan: 620 mL.

**Step 1** **Connect pain to retention:** restlessness and pain with 620 mL in the bladder is acute urinary retention.

**Step 2** **Threshold crossed:** no void by 6–8 hours and a scan above 400 mL.

**Step 3** **SBAR and decompress:** request catheterisation now; multimodal non-opioid analgesia; avoid further opioid.

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 18

**A 28-year-old man with active Crohn’s proctitis (six bloody stools a day, raised calprotectin) requests removal of prolapsing grade III haemorrhoids and oedematous tags. What is correct?**

- **A.** Immediate excisional haemorrhoidectomy and tag excision
- **B.** Defer haemorrhoid procedures and control the IBD first
- **C.** Stapled haemorrhoidopexy
- **D.** Band all three columns today

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Excision in active Crohn’s disease risks non-healing wounds, stenosis, and sepsis.
- ✅ **B** **Correct.** The AGA 2026 update advises delaying haemorrhoid treatment until complete remission of IBD.
- ❌ **C** A stapler in inflamed rectum risks dehiscence, sepsis, and fistula.
- ❌ **D** Banding inflamed mucosa risks ulceration, bleeding, and sepsis.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 18

1. ⚠️ Treating post-operative restlessness with opioid when the bladder is full.
2. ⚠️ Missing concealed bleeding under the patient.
3. ⚠️ Recommending antiseptic sitz baths.
4. ⚠️ Handing over “monitor closely” without numbers.
5. ⚠️ Discharging without teach-back and written triggers.
