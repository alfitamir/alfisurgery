## 1. Colonoscopy Red Flags — Summary

Haemorrhoids and colorectal cancer frequently coexist. Full details are in Chapter 08; the essentials are below.

**Table 16.1 — When the colon must be examined before blaming haemorrhoids**

| Red flag                             | Trigger                                                                                                 | Why                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Age                                  | At or above the national screening start age without up-to-date screening                               | Neoplasia risk rises with age (USPSTF 2021)    |
| Iron-deficiency anaemia              | Any man, postmenopausal woman, or unexplained in a premenopausal woman                                  | Bidirectional endoscopy is standard (BSG 2021) |
| Constitutional or bowel-habit change | Weight loss; persistent change in frequency or consistency; tenesmus; dark blood mixed with stool       | Classic features of colorectal cancer or IBD   |
| Family or personal history           | First-degree relative with colorectal cancer, Lynch syndrome, polyposis, IBD, or prior advanced adenoma | Lower threshold, earlier colonoscopy           |
| Persistent bleeding or positive FIT  | Bleeding persisting after 4–6 weeks of treatment; FIT ≥ 10 µg Hb/g                                      | The source may not be anal (NICE DG56)         |

## 2. Cross-Specialty Medication Pitfalls

**Table 16.2 — Six high-alert medication traps (full details in Appendix B)**

| Drug class                                                                               | Pitfall                                                                                 | Prevention rule                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Topical GTN + PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, vardenafil, tadalafil, avanafil) or riociguat | Profound hypotension, syncope, myocardial ischaemia                                     | 🔴 No PDE5 inhibitor within 24 h (sildenafil, vardenafil) or 48 h (tadalafil) of GTN; prefer topical diltiazem or nifedipine (ASCRS 2023)                          |
| SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin)            | Fournier’s gangrene (FDA 2018 warning); euglycaemic ketoacidosis                        | 🔴 Stop immediately if perineal infection is suspected and check ketones; stop 3 days (ertugliflozin 4 days) before elective surgery per labelling                 |
| Anticoagulants and antiplatelets                                                         | Delayed bleeding (typically days 5–10) after banding or haemorrhoidectomy               | 🟡 Agree a peri-procedural plan with the prescriber [L]; IRC or sclerotherapy if anticoagulation cannot stop; IRC preferred in significant coagulopathy (AGA 2026) |
| Opioids                                                                                  | Hard stool, fissure, urinary retention, impaction                                       | 🟡 Multimodal analgesia; laxative from day 1 with every regular opioid                                                                                             |
| Topical corticosteroids                                                                  | Atrophy, dermatitis, infection, masking of cancer                                       | 🟡 No more than 2 weeks at a time (AGA 2026)                                                                                                                       |
| NSAIDs                                                                                   | Bleeding with antithrombotics; AKI; may aggravate IBD; avoid from 20 weeks of pregnancy | 🟢 Good opioid-sparing analgesia in healthy adults; avoid in CKD, peptic ulcer, therapeutic anticoagulation, and late pregnancy                                    |

**Algorithm 16.1 — The safety net**

```text
[PATIENT WITH AN ANORECTAL COMPLAINT]
|
STEP 1 - LIFE-THREATENING?
|  Fournier's signs, abscess with fever, post-banding triad,
|  bleeding > 1 pad/h or shock index > 1, gangrenous
|  prolapse, perforation, cauda equina, neutropenic sepsis
|     -> YES: RED - call surgery now; resuscitate in parallel
|
STEP 2 - COLON OR CANCER RED FLAGS?
|  age/screening, IDA, weight loss, bowel-habit change,
|  family history, IBD, mass, non-healing lesion, FIT+
|     -> YES: YELLOW - colonoscopy / biopsy pathway first
|
STEP 3 - MEDICATION TRAPS?
|  PDE5i/riociguat + GTN, SGLT2i, antithrombotics,
|  opioids, long-term steroids, NSAIDs
|     -> fix before prescribing or referring
|
STEP 4 -> GREEN: evidence-based clinic or ER management
          with a dated review (4-8 weeks)
```

## 4. Role-Targeted Action Boxes

> **🚑 ER DOCTOR ACTION BOX**
>
> - **Check haemoglobin, MCV, and ferritin in every chronic bleeder.** A patient with Hb 82 g/L and MCV 68 fL who says “my piles bleed a little” needs urgent colonic and upper GI evaluation, even if grade II haemorrhoids are visible.

> **🩺 FAMILY MEDICINE / GP CLINIC BOX**
>
> - **Audit the medication list before any anorectal prescription:** PDE5 inhibitors and riociguat (before GTN), SGLT2 inhibitors (diabetics with perineal pain), antithrombotics (before banding), and opioids, iron, verapamil, and anticholinergics (constipation).

> **🏥 ER & WARD NURSE CARE AND SBAR BOX**
>
> - **Triage post-procedure returns as high acuity.** Any patient returning within 14 days of banding or haemorrhoidectomy with fever above 38.0 °C, difficulty passing urine, or heavy bleeding bypasses the waiting room for immediate observations and surgical notification.

> **💊 CROSS-SPECIALTY PRESCRIBING TRAP**
>
> - **Oral iron without a laxative** causes hard stools that tear fissures and worsen prolapse. Pair iron with a stool softener, or use IV iron when indicated — and never let iron replace investigation.

> **⛔ NEVER DO THIS**
>
> - Never accept the patient’s self-diagnosis of “piles” without examination.
> - Never prescribe GTN without asking about PDE5 inhibitors and riociguat.
> - Never renew steroid creams beyond 2 weeks without re-examination.

> **📝 COMMON DOCUMENTATION ERROR**
>
> - **“Known piles — repeat prescription”** with no examination, red-flag screen, or medication review.

**📨 REFERRAL URGENCY BOX**

| Urgency      | Clinical trigger                               | Who and how fast              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 🔴 EMERGENCY | Any Step 1 feature in Algorithm 16.1           | Call surgery now              |
| 🟡 URGENT    | Any Step 2 red flag                            | Colonoscopy or biopsy pathway |
| 🟢 ROUTINE   | Failure of conservative care without red flags | Surgical or specialist clinic |

## The Patient in Front of You — Chapter 16 Mini-Case

> **🧑‍⚕️ THE PATIENT IN FRONT OF YOU**
>
> A 61-year-old retired teacher asks for a repeat prescription of hydrocortisone suppositories for “his usual haemorrhoids”. On questioning, his stools have become narrower over 3 months and he has lost 5 kg. Blood tests show Hb 104 g/L, MCV 73 fL, and ferritin 8 µg/L. Anoscopy shows grade II haemorrhoids without active bleeding.

**Step 1** **Reject the assumption:** grade II haemorrhoids do not explain iron-deficiency anaemia, weight loss, and a change in stool calibre.

**Step 2** **Count the red flags:** age, IDA, weight loss, bowel-habit change.

**Step 3** **Act:** careful DRE for a low rectal mass; stop the steroid suppositories; urgent bidirectional endoscopy on the suspected-cancer pathway; iron replacement with a stool softener.

## Board-Style Question — Chapter 16

**A 59-year-old man with erectile dysfunction is prescribed topical 0.4% GTN for a chronic posterior fissure. Which home medication is contraindicated with it?**

- **A.** Amlodipine 5 mg daily
- **B.** Tadalafil 20 mg as needed
- **C.** Psyllium daily
- **D.** Diosmin–hesperidin twice daily

**Answer and explanation**

- ❌ **A** Amlodipine may add mild dizziness but is not contraindicated.
- ✅ **B** **Correct.** Tadalafil (a PDE5 inhibitor) with a nitrate can cause severe hypotension; avoid tadalafil within 48 hours of GTN. Switch to topical diltiazem 2% or nifedipine 0.2–0.3%.
- ❌ **C** Psyllium is recommended for fissure.
- ❌ **D** Flavonoids do not interact with nitrates.

## Five Most Dangerous Mistakes — Chapter 16

1. ⚠️ Repeating haemorrhoid prescriptions for a patient with iron-deficiency anaemia.
2. ⚠️ Prescribing GTN to a patient taking tadalafil.
3. ⚠️ Missing Fournier’s gangrene in a patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor.
4. ⚠️ Banding a patient on a DOAC without a plan.
5. ⚠️ Leaving a post-banding return in the waiting room.
