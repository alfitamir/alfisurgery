# APPENDIX D — POST-PROCEDURE DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS

<small>Printable patient sheets. Plain language. The return thresholds are identical on every sheet so that patients and staff learn one set of numbers.</small>

## D.1 Rubber band ligation (banding)

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | A dull ache or feeling of fullness for 1–3 days. A little bleeding when the band falls off, usually within 5–10 days. |
| Pain control | Regular paracetamol; ibuprofen if your doctor allows. Avoid codeine if you can — it causes constipation. |
| Bowel care | Fibre, plenty of water, and a stool softener. Do not strain. |
| Washing | Warm-water baths or showers are fine. No antiseptics in the water. |
| Special warning | **Pain that gets worse after day 2, with fever or difficulty passing urine, is an emergency** — say that you have had banding. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.2 Injection sclerotherapy or infrared coagulation

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Mild discomfort for a day; slight spotting for a few days. |
| Pain control | Paracetamol as needed. |
| Bowel care | Fibre, water, and do not strain. |
| Washing | Normal showers; warm-water baths if sore. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.3 Excision of a thrombosed external haemorrhoid

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Soreness for several days; small amounts of bleeding or ooze from the wound. |
| Pain control | Regular paracetamol, plus ibuprofen if allowed. |
| Bowel care | Fibre, water, and a stool softener from today. |
| Wound and baths | Remove the dressing after the first bowel movement or the next day; warm-water baths 2–3 times a day and after bowel movements; pat dry; wear a pad. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.4 Incision and drainage of a perianal abscess

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Discharge from the wound for 1–2 weeks; gradual healing from the inside. Routine daily packing is usually not needed. |
| Pain control | Regular paracetamol, plus ibuprofen if allowed. |
| Bowel care | Soft stools: fibre, water, a stool softener. |
| Wound and baths | Shower or warm-water bath 2–3 times a day and after bowel movements; absorbent pad; no antiseptics. |
| Follow-up | If discharge or a lump returns after healing, you may have a fistula — see your surgeon. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.5 Fissure ointment treatment

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Pain usually improves within 1–2 weeks; full healing takes 6–8 weeks. |
| How to use | A pea-sized amount on a gloved fingertip to the edge of the anus, usually twice daily, **for the full 6–8 weeks**. |
| GTN ointment | Headache is common at first. **Do not use sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, or avanafil** while using GTN. |
| Bowel care | Soft stools every day: fibre, water, a stool softener. |
| When to return | Not healed after 6–8 weeks; fever, swelling, or throbbing pain (possible abscess). |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.6 Haemorrhoidectomy

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Pain for 1–2 weeks, worst with bowel movements; spotting for several weeks; some swelling. |
| Pain control | Regular paracetamol plus an NSAID if allowed; prescribed ointment; minimal opioid. |
| Bowel care | Stool softener daily and fibre; aim to open your bowels by day 2–3. |
| Wound and baths | Warm-water baths 2–3 times a day and after bowel movements; pad in underwear. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |

## D.7 Pilonidal abscess drainage

| Topic | What to do |
|---|---|
| What is normal | Discharge for 1–2 weeks; slow healing from the base. |
| Pain control | Paracetamol, plus ibuprofen if allowed. |
| Wound care | Daily shower; dressing changes as advised; keep dry; avoid long sitting on hard surfaces. |
| After healing | Your surgeon may suggest hair removal or a planned operation to prevent recurrence. |
| 🚨 Seek urgent care | **Go to the emergency department now** if: bleeding soaks **more than 1 pad in an hour** or you pass clots; **temperature above 38.0 °C** or shivering; you **cannot pass urine for 6–8 hours** or your lower tummy is painful and swollen; pain is **getting worse** instead of better, or is not controlled by your painkillers. |
