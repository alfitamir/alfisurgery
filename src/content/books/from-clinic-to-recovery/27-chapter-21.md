# Chapter 21 — Corticosteroids and Other Immunosuppressive Medicines

Corticosteroids are among the most widely used medicines, for asthma, arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, skin conditions, and many others. Patients who take them for weeks or longer may not be able to produce enough of their own cortisol under the stress of surgery, and can develop an adrenal crisis: a sudden, life-threatening drop in blood pressure. Steroids also slow wound healing and increase infection. Many patients do not think of inhalers, creams, joint injections, or a course that finished recently as “steroids,” so careful questioning is essential. This chapter explains how to identify patients at risk, how to give steroid cover, and how to teach patients to protect themselves.

**STEP 1 Identifying risk and explaining the plan**

## 21.1 Who is at risk

Patients with primary or secondary adrenal insufficiency are at high risk of adrenal crisis around surgery. In addition, a daily dose of prednisolone of 5 mg or more, or its equivalent, taken for 4 weeks or more, may suppress the body’s own cortisol production, whether taken by mouth or by inhaled, intranasal, intra-articular, or topical routes [1]. Patients who have recently stopped long-term steroids may still be at risk, and the 2024 European and Endocrine Society guideline gives detailed advice on assessment [2]. ## 21.2 Steroid cover for surgery

Patients at risk should take their usual steroid dose on the morning of surgery and receive additional hydrocortisone according to the type of procedure (Figure 21.1). For major surgery, the 2020 UK guideline recommends hydrocortisone 100 mg intravenously at induction, followed by 200 mg over 24 hours by infusion, or 50 mg every 6 hours, while the patient is nil by mouth or vomiting, and then double the usual oral dose for 48 hours or up to a week before returning to the usual dose [1]. For body surface and intermediate surgery, the same induction dose is followed by double the usual oral dose for 48 hours [1]. Endocrinology advice should be sought for patients with primary adrenal insufficiency, very high doses, or complex courses.

Guidance differs for some groups. A 2022 rheumatology guideline for hip and knee replacement advises continuing the usual daily steroid dose, rather than giving extra stress doses, in patients taking steroids for rheumatic diseases who do not have known adrenal insufficiency [3]. Each hospital should agree one approach with its endocrinology and anesthesia teams.

## 21.4 Wound healing and other immunosuppressive medicines

Long-term steroids slow wound healing, thin the skin, and increase infection. Reduce the dose before elective surgery only with the prescribing specialist, never abruptly. Other immunosuppressive medicines, such as methotrexate, biologic therapies for arthritis or inflammatory bowel disease, and transplant medicines, need an individual plan with the prescribing specialist; some are continued, and some are paused for one dosing cycle before planned surgery [3]. Transplant medicines should never be stopped without specialist advice.

**STEP 2 Recovery, sick-day rules, and Ramadan**

## 21.5 After surgery and at home

- **Hospital.** Continue intravenous hydrocortisone while the patient is nil by mouth or vomiting, then double the oral dose as planned.

<!-- -->

- **Discharge.** Give a written plan for when to return to the usual dose, and check that the patient has a steroid emergency card [4].

- **Sick-day rules.** If the patient develops a fever, an infection, or another illness needing bed rest, they should double their usual steroid dose and seek advice; if they are vomiting and cannot keep tablets down, they need an injection and urgent medical care.

- **Wounds.** Check wounds closely; healing may be slower.

## 21.6 Ramadan

Patients who take steroid replacement for adrenal insufficiency are at higher risk when fasting and should discuss fasting with their endocrinologist, who may adjust the timing or type of steroid. No patient should fast while recovering from surgery or while unwell.

**STEP 3 Questions patients ask**

**“Why do I need extra steroid for the operation?”**

Your body may not make enough of its own steroid under the stress of surgery, because you have taken steroids for some time. Extra steroid keeps your blood pressure safe [1].

**“Should I take my steroid on the morning of surgery?”**

Yes. Never miss it. We will give you extra on the day.

**“I use a steroid inhaler. Does that count?”**

It can, especially at higher doses. Tell us about all inhalers, nasal sprays, creams, and joint injections [1].

**“Will steroids affect my wound?”**

They can slow healing and increase infection. We check your wound closely; do not reduce your dose yourself.

**“What is a steroid emergency card?”**

A card that tells any doctor that you take steroids and may need an urgent injection if you are very unwell. Carry it at all times [4].

**“What should I do if I get sick at home?”**

If you have a fever or infection, double your steroid dose and call your doctor. If you are vomiting and cannot keep tablets down, go to the emergency department at once and show your card.

**“Can I fast in Ramadan?”**

Not while recovering from surgery. If you take steroid replacement for adrenal insufficiency, discuss fasting with your endocrinologist first.

**Why steroids matter for your operation**

If you have taken steroids for some time, your body may not make enough of its own steroid during an operation or illness. This can make your blood pressure fall dangerously. Extra steroid around the operation keeps you safe.

**My steroid plan**

|                               | **What to take**                                           |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| **My usual steroid and dose** |                                                            |
| **Morning of surgery**        | Take your usual dose. Do not miss it.                      |
| **In hospital**               | Extra steroid given by the team                            |
| **After going home**          | Double dose for \_\_\_\_\_\_ days, then back to usual dose |

**Golden rules**

- **Never stop your steroid suddenly.**

- Tell every doctor, nurse, and dentist that you take steroids.

- Carry your steroid emergency card at all times.

- Tell us about inhalers, nasal sprays, creams, and joint injections too.

**If you are ill at home: sick-day rules**

- **Fever or infection:** double your steroid dose and call your doctor.

- **Vomiting and cannot keep tablets down:** go to the emergency department now and show your card. You need an injection.

**Ramadan**

- Do not fast while recovering from surgery or while unwell.

- If you take steroid replacement, ask your endocrinologist before fasting.

**Warning signs: when to get help**

| **Level** | **What you notice**                                                                              | **What to do**                                              |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **GREEN** | Tiredness for a few days                                                                         | Take your steroids as planned.                              |
| **AMBER** | Fever or infection; a red or leaking wound                                                       | Double your dose if advised, and call your doctor today.    |
| **RED**   | Vomiting and cannot keep tablets down; dizzy or fainting; severe weakness; tummy pain; confusion | Go to the emergency department now. Show your steroid card. |
