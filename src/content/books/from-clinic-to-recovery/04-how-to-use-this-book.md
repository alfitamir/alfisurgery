# How to Use This Book

## Who the book is for

- **Surgeons and consultants** who see patients in clinic and obtain consent.

<!-- -->

- **Residents, registrars, fellows, and physician assistants** who prepare patients on the ward, answer bedside questions, and write discharge instructions.

- **Nurses** in surgical clinics, pre-anesthesia assessment, and surgical and enhanced recovery wards, who teach patients and families before admission and before discharge.

- **Quality and patient-experience leads** who design consent processes, patient information, and discharge pathways.

## How the book is organized

Part I teaches three foundation skills once: running a consultation that ends in informed consent, explaining enhanced recovery, and supporting spoken advice with printed and digital material. Parts II and III apply these skills to individual operations and to patients who need adjusted advice. Part IV covers the return to normal life. The appendices contain templates that can be copied for clinical use.

## The five-step chapter

Every procedure chapter in Parts II and III follows the same five steps, so that you can find what you need quickly.

| **Step**   | **What it contains**                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Step 1** | **Explaining the disease and the operation.** A counseling script in plain language, with analogies, the reason for surgery, how the operation is done, and the alternatives.                                          |
| **Step 2** | **Preparation, medicines, and recovery.** Fasting, medicines to stop and continue, smoking and alcohol, wound care, and a timeline for walking, swimming, driving, flying, work, sport, prayer postures, and intimacy. |
| **Step 3** | **Questions patients ask.** Short, honest answers to the most frequent questions before and after surgery.                                                                                                             |
| **Step 4** | **Risks, warning signs, and consent.** Material risks grouped by frequency, a consent checklist, and green, amber, and red warning signs.                                                                              |
| **Step 5** | **Patient education guide.** A standalone handout, written at about a sixth-grade reading level (age 11 to 12), that can be given directly to patients and families.                                                   |

## The seven recovery domains

Each patient guide covers the same seven domains, which makes the guides easy to compare and easy for patients to follow:

1.  Pain and pain medicines.

<!-- -->

1.  Wound, dressing, and drain care.

2.  Diet, constipation, and gas.

3.  Sleep, showering, bathing, and clothing.

4.  Return to work, study, driving, swimming, travel, sport, and prayer.

5.  Follow-up, test results, and a personal instruction card.

6.  Possible complications and emergency warning signs.

## Warning signs: green, amber, red

Warning signs are grouped into three levels in every chapter and every patient guide, and the same system is used for telephone follow-up (Chapter 3).

| **Level** | **Meaning**                  | **What the patient should do**                           |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Green** | Expected after the operation | Nothing urgent. Follow the guide; reassurance only.      |
| **Amber** | Needs assessment today       | Call the clinic or contact number the same day.          |
| **Red**   | Possible emergency           | Go to the emergency department or call an ambulance now. |

## Using the timelines

Recovery timelines in this book are typical ranges for uncomplicated recovery. They are starting points for a conversation, not promises. Adjust them for the operation actually performed, the patient’s general health, and the findings at surgery, and record the individual plan on the personal instruction card (Appendix A).

## Boxes and conventions

Four kinds of box appear in the chapters. _Learning objectives_ open each chapter. _Script_ boxes give suggested words, which readers should adapt to their own style and language. _Caution_ boxes, with an amber border, flag safety points that are easy to miss. _Key points_ close each chapter and can be used for teaching. Risks are expressed as natural frequencies (for example, “3 out of 100”), and the risk bands used throughout are defined in Section 1.5.
